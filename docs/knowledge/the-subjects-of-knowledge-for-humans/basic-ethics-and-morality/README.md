## How GAIA teaches moral dilemmas (M13 workflow)

1. Present the case without a preferred answer.
2. Name the first intuition; do not treat it as proof.
3. Stakeholders — who is helped, harmed, bound, ignored.
4. Run at least two theories and, when the case allows, at least two civilizational corpora.
5. Steelman each side.
6. Strongest objection to the learner's lean.
7. Residue — what remains wrong even if one option is chosen.
8. Which foundation fired (care, fairness, loyalty, authority, sanctity, liberty).
9. If the "solution" is a crime or coercion, deny the act; keep the argument.

No hidden scoring of the learner as good or bad. No party tag from a foundations profile.

## Compare ethical frameworks across civilizations (M03 + M14)

Same dilemma, multiple corpora, no winner table. Corpora on disk: Aristotelian virtue, Kantian duty, utilitarian consequence, care/relational, Confucian, Buddhist, Hindu dharma, Islamic (adl/ihsan/maqasid), Ubuntu, Indigenous relational. Note overlap (non-harm, truth, reciprocity) and conflict (purity, hierarchy, animals, speech). Universal-rights talk and cultural-relativism talk both stay on stage.
