This folder is inside the Human Knowledge catalog. It is not a new plane.

Civics taught as org charts produces recall without citizenship. GAIA treats Basic Civics as four dimensions, three civic habits, and fifteen modules. Knowledge, skill, disposition, and ethics move together.

GAIA does not rank parties or candidates. Party preference is not stored. Democracy is taught as a rights-and-law system under strain and comparison; other systems are described, not caricatured. Civic action is lawful and learner-chosen. Simulation is not covert organizing.

## Science the system is bound to

### Four dimensions (simultaneous)

Civic knowledge · Civic skills · Civic dispositions · Civic ethics.

Knowledge without skill is passive. Skill without disposition is self-serving. All three without ethics can work the system against the common good.

### Habits

Knowledge · Habits of mind (deliberation, perspective) · Habits of heart (empathy, belonging). Citizenship is practiced, not only recited.

### Transcendent thinking

Connect a concrete civic fact to system, ethics, and identity. Hold more than one view while aiming at shared rules. Emotion is in play; rage is not a method.

### Nonpartisan rule

Map candidate positions to a learner's stated values when asked. Do not pick a winner. Do not store a party tag on the civic profile.

## Module contracts

### M01 — Civics Diagnostic Engine

Four dimensions; transcendent thinking; digital citizenship; civic identity and agency. Jurisdiction is labeled (US-default content is marked US, not universal). No party field. Gate: `ask`. Vault-scoped.

### M02 — Foundations of Democracy System

Meanings of democracy. Comparison with other systems as descriptions. Principles: popular sovereignty, rule of law, separated powers, federalism, rights, majority-with-minority, elections, expression, press, independent courts, civilian control of arms. Intellectual history from Athens through suffrage and decolonization — not a single origin myth.

### M03 — Government Structures System

Branches in interaction. Presidential, parliamentary, semi-presidential; federal, unitary, confederal. Local through supranational. Electoral systems. How a bill moves. Courts and juries.

### M04 — Rights & Responsibilities System

US Bill of Rights labeled US. UDHR and related instruments as another corpus. Duties and rights in tension, argued not sloganned.

### M05 — Civic Participation System

Electoral and non-electoral paths. Parties as institutions, not teams to join by default. Action is learner-chosen and lawful.

### M06 — Civic Reasoning & Deliberation System

Fact → implication. Structured controversy. Civic argument with counter. False balance is named. Uses literacy writing tools.

### M07 — Constitutional Literacy System

US Constitution as one document among others (Magna Carta through UN Charter). Interpretation schools presented as schools.

### M08 — Digital Citizenship System

News literacy; mis- vs disinformation; synthetic media; algorithms; privacy and platform power as civic questions.

### M09 — Global Civics System

UN and international courts as contested institutions. Comparative government and backsliding as sourced claims. Global citizenship as an idea, not a replacement passport.

### M10 — Civic History System

Rights struggles and methods. Failures included. Uses History M07.

### M11 — Civic Issues & Policy System

Structured controversies. Trade-offs explicit. No silent policy winner.

### M12 — Civic Simulation & Action System

Mock legislature, court, election. Lawful action projects. Harm, doxxing, or illegal disruption is deny.

### M13 — Civic Identity & Agency System

Local, national, global civic selves. Efficacy. Courage as speech and lawful dissent, not extra-legal force.

### M14 — Equity & Justice in Civics System

Inclusion and exclusion history. Indigenous sovereignty and treaties. Immigrant status as law. Justice frames do not override Layer 9.

### M15 — Civics Analytics & Adaptive Engine

Four-dimension growth; agency; digital literacy; simulation log; misconception set. No hidden rank. No party tag. No plaintext profile in the public audit log.
