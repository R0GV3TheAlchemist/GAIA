This folder is inside the Human Knowledge catalog. It is not a new plane.

Geography taught as capitals produces recall without spatial thought. Place is the most neglected concept in world curricula. GAIA treats Basic Geography as three concepts, six thinking dimensions, two domains, and fifteen modules. Space, place, and boundaries stay in play together.

Home-place data is vault-scoped. GIS is analysis literacy, not covert tracking.

## Science the system is bound to

### Three concepts

| Concept | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Space | Where? Coordinates, distance, area |
| Place | What does it mean to be here? |
| Boundaries | Who controls this? Who belongs? |

Space becomes place through boundaries. Boundaries can also break place.

### Six thinking dimensions

Spatial thinking · Place · Scale · Interconnection · Environmental interaction · Change over time.

Content without these is a gazetteer. Thinking without content is empty.

### Two domains (integrated)

Physical geography · Human geography. Climate change is the joint case.

### Spatial cognition (design notes)

Novel places encode more strongly than review of the familiar. Spatial reasoning is active. Navigation and planning share networks. Maps that attach to memory last longer than abstract sheets.

### GIS

Geospatial tools are core practice, not decoration. Field and satellite data stay cap-scoped. GeoAI does not silently surveil.

## Module contracts

### M01 — Geography Diagnostic Engine

Spatial thought; map reading; physical and human knowledge; place knowledge; six dimensions; GIS literacy; environmental literacy. Output: `geography-profile.schema.json`. Gate: `ask`. Vault-scoped.

### M02 — Spatial Thinking Foundation System

Absolute and relative location; direction; mental maps and their bias; distance and scale shift; time-distance; pattern types; proximity, connectivity, interaction, hierarchy, diffusion.

### M03 — Map Literacy System

Title, legend, scale, compass, grid; map types; contours; lat/long, GPS, time zones, key parallels. Thematic maps. Projections and what they distort, including whose center. Digital layers. Political use of the frame is taught, not hidden.

### M04 — Physical Geography System

Earth structure; tectonics; hazards; weathering and erosion; river, coast, glacial, desert, karst. Weather vs climate; atmosphere; temperature and precipitation controls; pressure and wind; currents; zones; climographs. Climate change evidence, uneven impact, justice, mitigation, adaptation — analysis, not a political whip. Water cycle and scarcity. Biomes, soil, succession. Hazard risk. Shares Earth science with Basic Science; this module keeps the spatial cut.

### M05 — Human Geography System

Population, pyramids, migration, displacement. Urban and rural change. Sectors, development measures, trade, globalization, tourism, agriculture, energy. Language, religion, landscape, diffusion, Indigenous cultures. State, nation, border, geopolitics, colonial lines, contested land. SDGs as spatial problems.

### M06 — Place Knowledge System

UN states and capitals as locational literacy, not the whole subject. Features and regional frames including Arctic and Antarctic. Spaced map drills. Disputed status is labeled disputed.

### M07 — Geographical Thinking Engine

Why here; distribution; association; prediction; place identity and contest; multi-scale view; physical–human and global links; determinism vs possibilism; justice; change over time.

### M08 — GIS & Geospatial Technology System

Layers, attributes, query, overlay, buffer. Public tools as literacy. Remote sensing and change detection. Web and story maps. Field GPS. Citizen mapping only to public projects. No ambient location store. No covert global watch.

### M09 — Geographical Inquiry System

Where / why there / what like / connected / changing / consequences / what should be done. Virtual field methods. Case set from many regions.

### M10 — Environmental Geography & Sustainability System

Impact geography; vulnerability; displacement; sea level; extremes. Footprint and planetary boundaries as models. Water, food, energy, minerals, forest, fish, land. Pollution including transboundary. Action stays lawful and non-coercive.

### M11 — Cultural & Indigenous Geography System

Land relation, TEK, names, wayfinding, Indigenous maps, rights, sacred landscapes. Contested territories with more than one map.

### M12 — Geographical Skills System

Climograph, pyramid, choropleth, scatter, flow. Intro stats and sampling. Photo and satellite read. Uses mathematics.

### M13 — Place-Based Learning System

User's place only with consent. Local projects. Sense of place. Comparison, not ranking of homes.

### M14 — Geographical Misinformation System

Projection myths; centering bias; documented falsehoods; disputed-border complexity; development stereotype. T0 stays visible.

### M15 — Geography Analytics & Adaptive Engine

Six-dimension growth; map and GIS tracks; domain knowledge; locational spacing; misconception set; scale links. No hidden rank. No plaintext home-place in the public audit log.
