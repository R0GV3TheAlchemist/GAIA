This folder is inside the Human Knowledge catalog. It is not a new plane.

History taught as names and dates produces recall without reasoning. GAIA treats Basic History as two knowledge types, seven thinking concepts, four document skills, and fifteen modules. Corroboration is first-class. A single national story is not the history.

AI may scaffold sources. AI does not invent dates, quotes, or causes. Generated historical claims stay flagged until checked.

## Science the system is bound to

### Two knowledge types (both required)

| Type | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Substantive | Events, people, places, periods, terms |
| Disciplinary | How claims are built from evidence |

### Seven historical thinking concepts

Significance · Cause and consequence · Continuity and change · Historical perspective · Evidence · Ethical dimension · Continuity (what does not change).

### Four document skills

Sourcing · Contextualizing · Close reading · Corroborating.

Curricula often starve corroboration. This system does not.

### Inquiry arc

Essential question → supporting questions → launch → investigate sources → synthesize.

### Learning methods

Retrieval, spacing, interleaving, chronological frame first. Place-based and culturally situated work. Empathy is perspective-taking, not pity and not excuse.

### Multiperspectivity

African, Asian, Indigenous, Latin American, Middle Eastern, Pacific, and European accounts of the same events sit side by side. Silence is a fact about the archive, not proof that nothing happened.

## Module contracts

### M01 — History Diagnostic Engine

Substantive knowledge; seven thinking concepts; four document skills; chronology; perspective; multiperspectivity; empathy as explanation of why; myth resistance. Output: `history-profile.schema.json`. Gate: `ask`. Vault-scoped.

### M02 — Chronological Framework System

Master frame from deep time to present. Human prehistory through contemporary. Sequence, period, duration, simultaneous-world maps, pace of change, vocabulary (BCE/CE, era, epoch). Parallel regional and thematic lines. Chronology before dense content.

### M03 — World History Content System

Substantive corpus, global by default: human origins and Neolithic change; ancient Afro-Eurasia and the Americas and Oceania; medieval worlds including Islamic Golden Age, African kingdoms, East and Southeast Asia, the Americas, Viking age; early modern encounter, Columbian exchange, slave trade, empires, resistance, Reformation, scientific revolution, Mughal, Ottoman, Ming/Qing, Tokugawa, Enlightenment; modern revolutions, industry, imperialism, abolition, world wars, Holocaust, decolonization; contemporary Cold War, rights movements, globalization, environment, digital age. Content is raw material for M04–M07, not a single story.

### M04 — Historical Thinking Engine

Trainers for all seven concepts. Significance criteria and whose significance. Causes: spark vs powder, intended vs not, counterfactual as probe not proof. Change and continuity including AHA continuity. Perspective and presentism detector. Evidence types, reliability vs usefulness, absence. Ethical judgment after context, not instead of it.

### M05 — Primary Source Laboratory

Document, image, oral, statistical, cartographic, artifactual sources. Marginalized and Indigenous sources included. HAPP/CREW sourcing. Context rebuild. Close read, implication, omission. Corroboration side-by-side. DBQ path. Uses literacy close-reading tools.

### M06 — Historical Empathy System

Not sympathy and not agreement. Reconstruct knowledge, options, belief, structure. Decision simulations under past constraints. Testimony with care. Historical fiction marked invented vs evidenced.

Difficult content (genocide, slavery, war) gets process space. No spectacle.

### M07 — Multiperspectival History System

At least three cultural angles on major events. Colonized views of empire. Non-Atlantic views of world wars. Workers on industry. Regional cores, not Europe-as-default. Women's, labor, enslaved, queer, disability, and children's histories as history, not appendix. Contested interpretation vs denial.

### M08 — Historical Inquiry Engine

Essential and supporting questions. QFT. Launch, investigate, synthesize, deliberate.

### M09 — Thematic History System

Power; trade and labor; belief; migration; technology; environment; gender and family; disease; art and ideas — each traced across time and region.

### M10 — Regional History Systems

Africa; Asia; the Americas; Europe; Middle East and North Africa; Oceania and the Pacific — each on its own terms, including before and after empire.

### M11 — Historical Misinformation & Myth System

Documented myth set with evidence. Online red flags. Revision vs denial. Propaganda as source. Memory, monuments, trauma. T0 dissent stays visible.

### M12 — History-Civics Connection System

Present issues with origins. Democracy and rights as historical struggles. Truth commissions, apology, reparation as cases. Local and family history. Citizenship, not party instruction.

### M13 — Digital History & Archives System

Pointers to public archives. Historical maps. Oral collections. AI adaptation of reading level is allowed. AI-invented fact is deny until verified.

### M14 — Historical Writing System

Thesis, evidence, counterargument. Genres: analytic, comparative, DBQ, narrative, biography, explanation. Accuracy check. Nuanced judgment flagged for human review. Uses literacy M07.

### M15 — History Analytics & Adaptive Engine

Thinking and document-skill tracks; chronology gaps; multiperspectivity exposure; misconception set; inquiry independence; reading-level adapt; spacing; period links; civic links. No hidden rank. No plaintext profile in the public audit log.
